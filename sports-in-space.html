Method & caveats

  • Clearance is structural margin + Earth ball apex ÷ g. Apex scales as the inverse of gravity, which is exact for vacuum ballistics. Volume is that clearance times the enclosure footprint; air mass is volume × 1.2 kg/m³ at 1 atm.
  • The model is calibrated against buildings that exist. At 1 g it returns 7 m of clearance for basketball, which is exactly what FIBA mandates, and 8 m for a hockey rink, which is what rinks are built to. That agreement at 1 g is the reason to trust the ratios everywhere else.
  • These are floors, not designs. A real roof spanning 150 m has structural depth of its own, which is why domed stadiums run 46–82 m where this model says 35. Real enclosures are larger than these numbers, never smaller.
  • Footprints are held at regulation size. If you also widen the field so athletes can use the longer strides low gravity allows, volume grows faster still — so treat every figure here as the optimistic case.
  • Air mass is quoted at 1 atm. A habitat running lower pressure scales it linearly: at 0.7 atm the tonnage drops by a third, which does nothing about the structure holding the volume up.
  • Zero gravity is excluded, not overlooked. At 0 g a ball has no apex and never returns, so required clearance is undefined and the slider stops at 0.05 g. In free fall the enclosure stops being a consequence of the sport and becomes a free design choice — a different question from this one.
